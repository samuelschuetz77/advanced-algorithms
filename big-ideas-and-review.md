## Course framing (CS 4230)

Algorithmic thinking allows us to leverage insightful observations, techniques, and solutions to other problems to arrive at efficient solutions and understanding for novel problems.

**Motivation:** The number of problems people want computers to solve is increasing, and the frequency and size of requests/data is increasing — so we need ways to compare and improve algorithms in light of the ability to scale.

## Main tools and ideas

- **Divide and conquer** — decomposes a problem into non-overlapping subproblems of the same type.
- **Dynamic programming** — solves problems by breaking them into a small number of repeatedly relevant subproblems of the same type.
- **Clever adaptations** of well-known algorithms, often used to address new problems.
- **Problem reductions** — express the essence of one problem instance as an instance of a different (often well-studied) problem type: graphs, trees, paths, flows, linear programming, boolean formulas.
- **Exhaustive search and backtracking** — systematically explore all possibilities to find solutions.
- **Greedy algorithms, heuristics, and approximation algorithms** — explore only some possibilities to efficiently find a good (sometimes provably optimal) solution.
- **Mathematical insights** — e.g. automatic differentiation and quantum algorithms — used to solve complex problems.
